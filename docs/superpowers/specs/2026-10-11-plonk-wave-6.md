# Plonk: sixth wave (the gaps left after the second parity audit)

Date: 2026-10-11. Builds on the earlier specs; where they differ, this one wins.

## Why

A second audit against WorldGuessr's code, after wave 5, found 115 of 154 rows matching, 22 partial, 5 missing and 12 out of scope. Its verdict: the modes, rules and numbers match; what remains is depth around the play. This wave closes the remaining gaps a player would notice and that a small free site can carry. Each item is marked **S** (server), **B** (browser) or **S+B**. The server builder adds the request and reply shapes for S+B items to `2026-10-11-plonk-wave-6-api.md` before either side builds them.

## A. Rules to align

1. **Win-farming curve (S).** Wins over the same opponent on the same UTC day: the first three pay in full, the fourth 0.75, the fifth 0.5, the sixth 0.25, the seventh and later nothing. The same factor trims the loser's loss, not only the winner's gain. The minimum change of 1 does not apply once the factor is 0.
2. **Spread of places in online world games (S+B, wherever the draw lives).** A public unranked, ranked or 2v2 game on the world map draws places from at least three continents when it has three or more rounds. Parties and other maps are unchanged.

## B. Features to add

3. **Country streak in ordinary play (B).** A singleplayer game on the world map counts a country streak in the background: each round whose guess lands in the right country adds one, a miss resets it. The round result shows the current streak when it is 1 or more, and that it ended when a streak of 2 or more is lost. The streak carries across world games in the same browser and feeds the account's best streak as the Country streak mode does. The separate Country streak mode stays.
4. **First visit (B).** A new visitor sees a welcome screen before the home pane: "Show me how" (the tutorial) or "Skip". The tutorial's rounds are on recognisable landmarks and each result adds one line of fact about the place. It ends on a screen offering Singleplayer, Daily challenge and Sign up. Never shown again once seen or skipped; replayable from the account pane as now.
5. **Account recovery (S+B).** There is no email, so recovery is by code. Sign-up shows a recovery code once (12 characters in groups of four), with "Copy" and a plain warning that it is the only way back in. The account pane can issue a new one, which replaces the old, after the current password. The sign-in form has "Forgot password": username, recovery code, new password. The code is stored hashed like a password, compared in constant time, rate-limited like sign-in, and replaced by a fresh one (shown once) when used. Accounts made before this wave have none until they issue one.
6. **Larger parties (S+B).** Capacity 40, so a school class fits. The browser polls a match every 1.5 seconds with up to 10 players, every 2.5 with up to 20 and every 4 above that; the server's rate limits allow for it. The lobby and roster stay usable at 40 (the review's phone roster chip already collapses).
7. **Party guests switch (S+B).** A host option "Allow guests", on by default. Off, a visitor without an account who opens the code is told to sign up.
8. **Queue wait estimate (S+B).** The queue screen shows a typical wait for that queue from the last 24 hours of matches found ("usually under a minute", "about 3 minutes"), or "no recent games" when there were none.
9. **2v2 record (S+B).** Wins, losses and ties in 2v2 on the account and the profile, beside the duel record.
10. **Reports (S+B).** A report carries a category (cheating, offensive name, offensive chat, other) with the optional note. The account pane gains "My reports": each with its target, date and status (open, action taken, dismissed). The moderation pane shows the category.
11. **Daily extras (S+B).** Each round on the daily result shows how far the player was above or below the day's average for that round, once at least five players have finished. A "Personal best" mark when the total beats the player's previous best daily.
12. **Hybrid map (B).** A fourth pin-map type: satellite with the place and street labels and borders of the streets style drawn over it.
13. **Maps: file upload (B).** The editor accepts a `.json` or `.csv` file of places, read in the browser by the same parser as the paste box.
14. **Small switches (S+B).** "Hide my last seen" on the account (friends then see only online or offline). "Show inactive players" on the leaderboards.
15. **Reactions and clicks (B).** A reaction sent in a match also floats briefly over the round screen near the sender's row, respecting reduced motion and the Hide chat setting. A soft click sound on buttons, under the same volume and Sound setting.

## C. Hardening (all S unless marked)

From an independent security review of `server/` and `api/`. It found no way to act as another account or guest, no SQL or HTML injection and no missing moderator check. What it found is leaderboard integrity and cheap griefing.

16. **Places and scale come from the server's knowledge, not the browser's word.** The server loads both place lists (`public/locations.google.json`, `public/locations.json`). A saved game, a daily, a claimed daily, a guesser or streak run and a match's posted places are accepted only when every place id exists in a list with those coordinates (or, for a community map, in that map). The scoring scale is derived on the server from the map (the world constant, the diagonal of the country's or region's places, or the community map's places); a `scaleKm` sent by the browser is ignored. A daily's places must be the ones its seed draws.
17. **Runs need a start and take time.** Daily, streak and guesser runs begin with a server-issued start (the daily already has one). A finish is refused when it arrives sooner than 2 seconds per answered round after its start, or names more rounds than the start allows. XP from solo modes is capped per account per UTC day at 20,000; play continues past the cap and the reply says XP was not added.
18. **Ranked duels.** The scale of a world game is the world constant whatever is posted. A ranked game cancelled because its places were never posted counts as a dodge by the player who was to post them.
19. **A guest can be the one who posts places** in a public game, or the longest-waiting account is chosen instead; a public game never stalls because a guest waited longest.
20. **Rating.** The matching window stops widening at ±400. The minimum change of 1 does not apply when the winner was rated 400 or more above the loser.
21. **Google viewer counter.** At most 10 viewers per address per UTC day, and the last third of the day's allowance is kept for signed-in accounts.
22. **Sessions end.** Each account carries a session version inside its token. Changing the password, using a recovery code, deleting the account and a ban raise it, which signs out every other browser. Sign-out raises nothing (it clears one cookie) unless the player chooses "Sign out everywhere" on the account pane (**S+B**).
23. **Bans hold everywhere.** A banned account is dropped when a party returns to its lobby or starts its next game, is not put back in a queue after an opponent's dodge, and cannot be queued as a partner. Maps of banned and deleted accounts are not served by slug either.
24. **Host powers go with the seat.** A host who has left the game under way has none of them.
25. **Moderator names cannot be registered.** Sign-up refuses a name listed in `MODERATORS`; list an account only after it exists.
26. **Only the current round's place is sent.** Match state carries places up to the round being played, not later ones.
27. **Limits.** Per-session limits on the match and social write actions that had none, generous enough for a class of 40 behind one address (key on the session or guest, not the address). An account's maps hold at most 25,000 places in total. A profile view counts once per viewer per day. IPv6 addresses are limited by their /64. Old `rate_limits` rows are purged.
28. **Small.** A NUL byte in any text field is a 400. The Origin check compares the scheme as well as the host.

Accepted and left: a player who reads the answer out of their own browser; a signed-in sign-in lock that someone with three addresses can trigger on purpose (the alternative is unlimited password guessing); guests cannot be reported (they have no rating, XP or chat to protect).

## Still out of scope, and why

Everything on wave 5's list, plus:

- **A bot team to fill 2v2 for newcomers, and the placement bot:** a guessing bot is a product of its own. On a quiet site the 2v2 queue will often not fill; parties and friends invites are the way to play it.
- **Party capacity of 200:** 40 is what polling can carry.
- **The daily "Tip" card, Spotlight maps, What's New pop-up, community links, maintenance mode:** editorial or community content this site does not have.
- **Maps of more than 5,000 places or more than 20 maps an account:** storage on a free database.
- **A per-country scoring table:** the diagonal of a map's places gives nearly the same scale and needs no table to maintain.
- **Typing indicator, compass rotate buttons, a custom date range on charts, an "avoid lower skill duels" setting:** small, and polling makes the first one costly.
- **Road names on by default and a local-midnight daily:** deliberate choices (the owner asked for no street names; one shared UTC day is fair to everyone).
